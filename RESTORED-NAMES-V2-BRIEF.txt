1. THE COMMISSION
-----------------
Examine EVERY instance of Jesus / Christ / the Christ / Messiah / Messias /
christs across the New Testament of both base translations - 3,120 of them -
INDIVIDUALLY. For each one, consult the underlying Greek, weigh the syntax and
the context, and decide the appropriate restored English rendering of the Name.

The owner's words, and the two halves that matter equally:

  "appropriately restore YahuShua HaMashiach"      <- fidelity
  "not just 'The Very HaMashiach', avoid awkward translations"   <- readability

A rendering that is technically defensible but reads as broken English is a
FAILED instance, not a stylistic quibble. See section 2 for why that is a
doctrinal judgment and not merely an editorial one.

This is explicitly NOT a batch job. A regex ruleset that happens to produce a
correct output for an instance is not an adjudication of that instance. The
deliverable is a per-instance decision record with reasoning, and only then a
machine that applies those records.


2. THE DOCTRINAL LINE (the boundary you must not cross in either direction)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Read Regarding_Sacred_Names in full before writing the rubric. Summarized, with
the tension stated plainly:

  AFFIRMED. YAHUWAH is the Father's name; YAHUSHUA HAMASHIACH is the Son's. The
  Volumes of Truth command calling upon His name "as it is, was, and has always
  been, and not as they presume it to be" (Death and Deliverance). This is the
  warrant for the whole edition.

  BOUNDED. The same teaching refuses name-exclusivism outright. It condemns the
  self-appointed teachers who assert that only by a certain name can one be
  saved. It states that He hears and knows regardless of the name or the tongue
  by which people have come to know Him. The restoration of a pure language is
  spoken of as future - "not yet." And Proclaim The Name of The Lord commands
  speaking His name as it is "and also in the tongue of the hearer."

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR YOUR TRANSLATION DECISIONS - this is the load-bearing part:

  (a) "In the tongue of the hearer" makes readable English a REQUIREMENT of the
      command, not a concession to it. A calque that no English reader can parse
      has not been spoken in the hearer's tongue. Awkwardness is therefore a
      conformance failure. This is the doctrinal ground for section 9.

  (b) The edition is a reverent reading aid, not a polemic. Nothing you write -
      not the verses, not the Settings explainer, not a commit message - may
      imply that other renderings are invalid or that this spelling is required
      for salvation. That posture is the exact thing the page condemns.

  (c) Restore what is HIS. Do not manufacture Hebrew elsewhere. Other bearers of
      the name keep their traditional rendering (the Matthew Study Bible's own
      precedent governs: only The Messiah's name was restored). Established
      English derivatives with independent meaning - "Christian", "antichrist" -
      are words of the hearer's tongue and stay.

  (d) When fidelity and readability genuinely conflict on a single instance,
      say so in that row's rationale and choose the reading that a reverent
      English reader would understand on first pass. Record the tension; do not
      silently pick one.

DELIVERABLE FROM THIS SECTION: the Settings > Bible Translation info panel
(TranslationInfoDesc in SettingsScreen.jsx) must carry a plain statement that
these editions are a personal reading aid, that the traditional renderings are
not thereby condemned, and that He is not bound to one spelling. If it does not
already say this, adding it is part of the final phase.


3. WHY V1 FAILED, AND WHY THE UNIT OF WORK MUST CHANGE
------------------------------------------------------
v1 (2026-07-12) mapped every standalone "Christ" to "HaMashiach". Because Ha IS
the definite article, that stranded English determiners in front of it:

  KJV John 7:26   "the very HaMashiach"   = "the very THE-Messiah"
  Acts 9:22       "very HaMashiach"
  John 1:25, 6:69 "that HaMashiach"

The 2026-07-20 re-audit fixed the defect class with a better rule (printed "the
Christ" -> HaMashiach; every other Christ -> bare Mashiach) and pinned it with a
corpus-wide guard. That rule is sound and the guard must survive. But a rule is
still the wrong UNIT: it decides a category, then hopes every member of that
category reads well. The owner is asking for the members to be decided.

Concretely, what per-instance work can do that no ruleset can:
  - choose between "HaMashiach" and "the Mashiach" for articular Greek based on
    which one the surrounding sentence actually wants
  - catch a verse where the restored token collides with a nearby word, a
    quotation frame, a parallelism, or a gloss
  - notice where the English word has no Greek counterpart at all (supplied)
  - read the whole verse aloud and reject a stiff result

The architecture in section 4 is what lets that coexist with the project's hard
rule that generated outputs are never hand-edited.


4. ARCHITECTURE: A DECISION LEDGER PLUS A DUMB APPLIER
------------------------------------------------------
This is the central design decision of v2. Do not deviate from it without
raising it with the owner first.

  tools/restored-nt-ledger.jsonl     <- 3,120 rows. ONE ROW PER INSTANCE.
                                        Human-readable, diffable, reviewable.
                                        THIS is the work product.

  tools/gen-restored-nt.mjs          <- rewritten. No linguistic rules left in
                                        it. It reads the ledger, applies each
                                        decision at its exact coordinates,
                                        asserts total coverage, emits overlays.

  src/data/bible-rnkjv.js            <- regenerated. Never hand-edited. Ever.
  src/data/bible-rkjv.js

Why this shape:
  - Every emitted byte traces to one adjudicated row with a rationale. That is
    the auditable proof that this was not a batch job.
  - Regeneration stays deterministic and reproducible, so Permanent Rules 1-2
    (ASCII hyphens, ASCII delimiters) and check_balance.py are never at risk
    from hand-editing a 180 KB data file.
  - The ledger is RESUMABLE STATE. A /goal that runs out of turns is re-run and
    picks up at the first row with decision == null.
  - Coverage is a NUMBER, which is exactly what a /goal evaluator can check.

Row schema (every field required unless marked optional):

  {
    "id":        "kjv|john|7|26|1",        translation|book|chapter|verse|occurrence
    "tr":        "nkjv" | "kjv",
    "book":      "john",                   bookId as used in NT[] in the generator
    "ch":        "7",
    "v":         26,
    "occ":       1,                        1-based index of this instance in the verse
    "surface":   "Christ",                 the exact English token as printed
    "before":    "...but look! He speaks boldly... Do the rulers know indeed that this is truly the very ",
    "after":     "? However we know where this Man is from...",
    "greek": {
      "found":       true,
      "form":        "Christos",           the TR word aligned to this instance
      "lemma":       "Christos",
      "parse":       "N-NSM",              full morphology from the TAGGED TR
      "case":        "nominative",
      "number":      "singular",
      "gender":      "masculine",
      "article":     "none",               none | ho | tou | to | ton | to(i) | ...
      "articleFn":   "n/a",                see section 6B: anaphoric | par-excellence |
                                           monadic | idiomatic-with-proper-name |
                                           omitted-after-preposition | generic | n/a
      "force":       "titular",            titular | onomastic | transitional (6C)
      "genitiveType": null,                subjective | objective | possessive | source |
                                           relationship | apposition | ambiguous (6E)
      "construction": null,                granville-sharp | set-formula | ot-quotation |
                                           naming-formula | gloss | vocative | null
      "witness":     "Stephanus1550",
      "variant":     null                  real TR-internal variance, if any (6L)
    },
    "syntax":    "predicate-nominative",   role in the English clause
    "speaker":   "narrator",               narrator | Yahushua | disciple | opponent |
                                           demon | roman-official | crowd | apostle-writing
    "supplied":  false,                    true when the English has no Greek counterpart
    "hebrew":    "Delitzsch reads HaMashiach here; construct forbids the article",
                                           optional but required for every titular Christos
    "decision":  "Mashiach",               MUST be one of the forms in section 8
    "verseOut":  "...that this is truly the very Mashiach? However we know...",
    "rationale": "English prints 'the very' before the token, so an article-bearing
                  form would strand a determiner; TR reads anarthrous Christos in a
                  predicate after 'this is truly'. Bare Mashiach keeps the sentence
                  intact and reads naturally.",
    "natural":   "pass",                   pass | tension | fail  (fail may not ship)
    "flag":      null                      optional: raise to the owner
  }

Rules for the ledger itself:
  - One JSON object per line. ASCII only in every field. No smart quotes, no en
    or em dashes anywhere in "verseOut" or "decision" - the emitted text must
    survive Permanent Rules 1-2 and check_balance.py verbatim.
  - "verseOut" holds the FULL restored verse text, not the token. Awkwardness is
    only visible in the whole sentence, so the whole sentence is the evidence.
  - Rows are never deleted. A reconsidered row is edited in place and its
    rationale states what changed and why.

6. THE GREEK: SOURCES AND THE PHENOMENA YOU MUST HANDLE
-------------------------------------------------------
This section is the technical heart of the commission. The owner asked for real
engagement with the underlying language. Parsing a word is not that. Knowing
WHICH Greek phenomenon you are looking at, and which of them are traps that
produce confident-sounding nonsense, is that.

6A. SOURCES, PROVENANCE, CACHING
  Base text is the TEXTUS RECEPTUS, because that is the text the KJV and NKJV
  New Testaments translate. Judging them against NA28/UBS5 would be a category
  error - you would be measuring a translation against a text it did not render.

  - Acquire a MORPHOLOGICALLY TAGGED TR, not a bare text. Hand-parsing 3,120
    instances invites silent error; a tagged text makes the morphology field
    mechanical and reliable and leaves your judgment for the parts that need it.
    If no tagged TR is obtainable, a tagged Byzantine/Majority text is an
    acceptable morphological aid PROVIDED the wording is checked against the TR
    per instance and any divergence is recorded.
  - Primary witness Stephanus 1550; cross-check Scrivener 1894 (the text
    reconstructed behind the KJV). Beza 1598 and Elzevir 1633 where they bear.
    All public domain.
  - Lexical and grammatical authorities that are public domain and citable:
    Thayer's Greek-English Lexicon, Strong's, Liddell-Scott-Jones, and
    A.T. Robertson's Grammar of the Greek New Testament (1919). Standard modern
    grammars may be cited by CATEGORY NAME without reproducing their text.
  - Hebrew witnesses for retro-translation: Delitzsch's Hebrew NT and
    Salkinson-Ginsburg. Both public domain.
  - Vendor everything under tools/tr-greek/ with PROVENANCE.md recording edition,
    source URL, retrieval date and license, per the VENDORED-LIBS.md convention.
  - CACHE IT. Never re-fetch per turn: a /goal loop that hits the network every
    turn is slow, fragile, and not reproducible offline.

6B. THE ARTICLE WITH PROPER NAMES - THE BIGGEST TRAP IN THIS PROJECT
  Koine routinely puts the article on personal names. "ho Iesous" is the normal
  way to write "Jesus" in Gospel narrative; it does NOT mean "the Jesus" and it
  does not make the name definite in any sense English marks. If you treat that
  article as English "the", you will manufacture determiners across hundreds of
  verses and reproduce the exact defect class that got v1 rejected.
    RULE: for Iesous, the presence of the article is almost never a reason to
    change the English rendering. Record it in articleFn as
    "idiomatic-with-proper-name" and move on.
  The article carries real weight for Christos, because Christos is a TITLE that
  is drifting toward a name (6C). Distinguish its functions and record which:
  anaphoric (points back to a previous mention), par excellence (the Anointed
  One above all others - the confessional uses), monadic (a one-of-a-kind
  referent), generic, and omitted-after-preposition (pure Greek syntax carrying
  no semantic weight - established in v1 and still true).

6C. CHRISTOS: THE TITULAR-TO-ONOMASTIC DRIFT
  This is the single most useful piece of context-awareness available to you,
  and it is why a per-instance reading beats a rule.
    In the Synoptics, John and Acts, Christos is overwhelmingly TITULAR - "the
    Anointed One" - and clusters in confession, interrogation and dispute
    scenes: is this man the Anointed, do you claim to be the Anointed, we have
    found the Anointed. There the title is doing titular work and an
    article-bearing restored form is usually right.
    In Paul it has largely lexicalized toward a PROPER NAME: frequently
    anarthrous, frequently in fixed pairs and prepositional formulas, behaving
    like a second name rather than a claim. There the bare name-like form is
    usually right.
    Hebrews, Peter and Revelation sit across the range.
  Record where each instance falls in greek.force as titular, onomastic, or
  transitional, and let that finding - not a global rule - drive the choice
  between an article-bearing and a bare rendering, subject always to section 9.

6D. PREDICATE NOMINATIVES AND CONFESSION FORMULAS
  "su ei ho Christos" (You are the Anointed) and its relatives are the high-water
  mark of titular force. Note that an anarthrous pre-verbal predicate nominative
  can still be definite in Greek, so absence of the article in a predicate is NOT
  evidence of a name-like use. Judge these by the confession context, not by the
  article alone.

6E. GENITIVE TYPE - AND THE OPEN QUESTION THIS SETTLES
  Roughly ninety verses read "of Christ". English flattens every genitive to
  "of", but the Greek relation differs and it changes what the phrase means:
  subjective (the faithfulness that He exercises), objective (faith directed
  toward Him), possessive, of source, of relationship, of apposition.
  The "pistis Christou" phrases (Romans 3:22 and 3:26, Galatians 2:16 twice,
  2:20, 3:22, Philippians 3:9, Ephesians 3:12) are the most contested genitive
  in New Testament scholarship. You are not being asked to settle that debate;
  you ARE being asked to notice that these instances are not interchangeable
  with "the gospel of Christ" and to adjudicate them as their own class.
  Record genitiveType per instance. The owner's ONE explicitly open decision -
  whether "of Christ" reads bare or article-bearing - is to be answered from
  this evidence, per instance first and as a reported pattern second.

6F. VOCATIVES AND DIRECT ADDRESS
  Christe occurs exactly once (Matthew 26:68). But direct address to Him using
  Iesou as a vocative is frequent - the demons and the petitioners in Mark 1:24,
  5:7, 10:47; Luke 4:34, 8:28, 17:13, 18:38 and their parallels. These are
  people speaking TO Him. Mark construction as vocative and let the restored
  form be the one a person would actually call out.

6G. GRANVILLE SHARP AND OTHER BOUND CONSTRUCTIONS
  The article-noun-kai-noun construction ("our great God and Savior Jesus
  Christ", Titus 2:13; "our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ", 2 Peter 1:11 and
  kin) binds the two nouns to a single referent. Your restored rendering must
  not break that unity or insert a determiner that splits it. Mark
  construction as granville-sharp and check the whole phrase reads as one.

6H. SET FORMULAS AND LITURGICAL PHRASES
  Epistolary greetings and benedictions, "our Lord Jesus Christ", "in Christ
  Jesus", "through Jesus Christ" are FIXED phrases repeated dozens of times.
  Two obligations: adjudicate them (they are not exempt), and then be
  CONSISTENT within the formula - a greeting that renders one way in
  Philippians and another way in Colossians reads as an error, not a nuance.
  Where you deliberately vary a formula, the rationale must say why.

6I. OLD TESTAMENT QUOTATION AND THE SEPTUAGINT SUBSTRATUM
  Where the New Testament quotes an Old Testament passage containing mashiach -
  Psalm 2:2 quoted at Acts 4:26, and the related Revelation 11:15 and 12:10
  phrases - the HEBREW source governs the restored form. Hebrew construct and
  suffixed forms cannot take the article, which is why "His Anointed" and "the
  Lord's Anointed" must render bare. Mark construction as ot-quotation and cite
  the source verse.

6J. SEMITIC RETRO-TRANSLATION
  For every titular Christos, record what Delitzsch does (greek.hebrew field).
  The governing Hebrew fact: in a construct chain the first noun cannot carry
  the article, and a pronominal suffix likewise blocks it. That is a grammatical
  prohibition, not a preference, and it is the reason several v1 exceptions
  exist. Salkinson-Ginsburg is a useful second witness where Delitzsch is
  surprising. These are witnesses, not authorities - the doctrine and the
  English sentence outrank them both.

6K. THE TWO TRANSLITERATIONS
  Messias appears only at John 1:41 and 4:25, where John transliterates the
  Aramaic and then GLOSSES it into Greek for his readers. Any rendering that
  translates the word into itself has failed. Mark construction as gloss and
  render the gloss by meaning.

6L. TEXTUAL VARIANCE INSIDE THE TR TRADITION
  Stephanus, Beza, Elzevir and Scrivener differ at a number of places on the
  presence of Iesous or Christos, especially in the longer "Lord Jesus Christ"
  readings. Record divergence in greek.variant. Do NOT change what the English
  base prints. You are restoring a name inside an existing translation, not
  re-editing its text.

6M. ALIGNMENT, SUPPLIED WORDS, AND THE SHADOW CENSUS
  - ALIGNMENT. Greek and English word order differ. Align by clause and lemma,
    never by position. Where alignment is not confident, set greek.found=false
    and say so. NEVER invent a form.
  - SUPPLIED WORDS. The KJV italicizes words with no Greek counterpart, and both
    translations sometimes supply the name where the Greek has only a pronoun or
    nothing at all. Mark supplied=true. A supplied "Jesus" is still restored -
    the referent is the Lord - but the rationale may not claim Greek evidence
    that is not there.
  - THE SHADOW CENSUS. The mirror case also exists: verses where the GREEK names
    Him but the English renders only "he" or "him". Those are places where the
    Name was lost in translation rather than obscured. Restoring them is OUT OF
    SCOPE - it would change base wording, which section 14 forbids - but you must
    COUNT them per book and report the total to the owner as a finding. He may
    want a separate commission for it. Do not act on it unasked.


7. THE ADJUDICATION RUBRIC (what "examining an instance" means)
--------------------------------------------------------------
For each row, in this order. Steps 3 through 6 are the language work the owner
is asking for; do not compress them.

  1. READ THE VERSE. The whole verse, before and after the token. In narrative,
     read the surrounding two verses - pronoun referents cross verse boundaries
     constantly in the Gospels and Acts. In the epistles, read to the end of the
     sentence, which is often several verses long.
  2. IDENTIFY THE REFERENT. Is this the Lord, or another bearer (Bar-Jesus,
     Justus, Joshua son of Nun)? Other bearers are UNCHANGED - decide and move on.
  3. PARSE THE GREEK. From the tagged TR: form, lemma, full morphology, case,
     number, gender, and the article if present. If the instance cannot be
     aligned with confidence, set greek.found=false and say so. Never invent.
  4. DIAGNOSE THE PHENOMENON. Walk section 6 and name what you are looking at:
       - Is the article idiomatic-with-a-proper-name (6B), or is it doing
         semantic work? For Iesous the answer is almost always the former.
       - Where does this Christos sit on the titular-to-onomastic range (6C)?
       - Is it a predicate in a confession (6D)? a genitive, and of which type
         (6E)? a vocative or direct address (6F)? part of a Granville Sharp
         construction (6G)? inside a set formula (6H)? an OT quotation whose
         Hebrew governs (6I)? one of the two glosses (6K)?
       - What does Delitzsch do with it, and does Hebrew construct or suffix
         grammar forbid the article here (6J)?
       - Do the TR editions agree at this point (6L)? Is the English word
         supplied with no Greek behind it (6M)?
     Record each finding in its field. A row whose greek block is filled but
     whose articleFn, force and construction are all null has been parsed, not
     diagnosed, and does not satisfy this step.
  5. IDENTIFY THE SPEAKER AND FRAME. Who is talking - the narrator, the Lord
     Himself, a disciple confessing, an opponent interrogating, a demon, a Roman
     official, an apostle writing? An outsider's naming formula ("Jesus who is
     called Christ") and a disciple's confession ("You are the Christ") are not
     the same speech act and should not automatically render the same way.
  6. CLASSIFY THE PRINTED ENGLISH. What determiner, adjective or possessive, if
     any, stands immediately before the token? Is it a name pair, a possessive,
     a naming formula? This is what constrains which forms are even available -
     the Greek informs the choice, the printed English bounds it. Section 9.
  7. CHOOSE from the closed vocabulary in section 8.
  8. WRITE THE FULL RESTORED VERSE into verseOut.
  9. RE-READ verseOut ALOUD AS A SENTENCE. Apply section 9. If it stumbles, go
     back to step 7 and choose differently. Mark natural=pass only when it reads.
 10. WRITE THE RATIONALE. It must reference the specific English words adjacent
     to this instance AND the specific Greek finding AND, where relevant, the
     phenomenon from section 6 that decided it. A rationale that could be pasted
     onto a different verse unchanged is not a rationale.

ON PACE AND REPEATS. Many instances are formulaic - the same greeting, the same
"our Lord Jesus Christ", the same narrative "and Jesus said". Those legitimately
go faster than a contested confession scene, and you should let them. What you
may NOT do is auto-fill them: each still gets its own row, its own referent
check, its own naturalness read, and a rationale naming the formula it belongs
to and confirming it behaves the same here. Speed comes from recognition, never
from skipping the read.

Work in verse order within a book. Do not skip ahead to the interesting ones -
the drift described in 6C is only visible if you walk the text in order.


8. THE CLOSED DECISION VOCABULARY
---------------------------------
Every "decision" value must be one of these. Introducing a new form requires
amending this section and flagging the owner - it is a doctrinal change, not an
implementation detail.

  YahuShua              the Lord, standing for "Jesus"
  YahuShua's            possessive
  YAHUSHUA              the naming verses and the cross inscriptions, where the
                        capitals carry the meaning "YAH Is Salvation"
                        (Matt 1:21, 1:25; Luke 1:31, 2:21; Matt 27:37; John 19:19)
  YahuShua HaMashiach   the full Name pairs, both "Jesus Christ" and "Christ Jesus"
  YahuShua HaMashiach's possessive of the pair
  HaMashiach            definite title where the English wants NO determiner
  the Mashiach          definite title where the English sentence wants one
  Mashiach              anarthrous, name-like, construct, suffixed, vocative,
                        appositive, predicate - any instance with no printed "the"
  Mashiach's            possessive
  Messiah / messiahs    where the English word is functioning as a common noun
                        ("false christs" -> "false messiahs")
  the Anointed          gloss-by-meaning, to avoid circular self-translation
                        (John 1:41, 4:25: "...which is translated, the Anointed")
  Joshua                Acts 7:45 and Heb 4:8 in the KJV only - referent is
                        Joshua son of Nun; NKJV already reads Joshua
  UNCHANGED             other bearers of the name; "Christian(s)"; "antichrist(s)"

NOTE the deliberate addition of "the Mashiach" alongside "HaMashiach". v1 forced
articular Greek to a single output. Per-instance work may choose whichever reads
better in that sentence. Both are faithful; only one will usually be natural.

STANDING DECISIONS TO RE-CONFIRM, NOT TO SILENTLY REVERSE. Each was decided by
the owner. If your per-instance work now argues against one, FLAG IT with the
evidence and let him call it. Do not just change it.
  - PAIR ORDER. "Christ Jesus" is normalized to "YahuShua HaMashiach", the
    commanded order (the VOT corpus writes it that way 121x and reversed 0x; the
    KJV itself already reversed 11 TR pairs, so order was never held inspired).
  - GENITIVE "of Christ" (~90 verses). v1 renders it bare, "gospel of Mashiach".
    The Greek is articular (tou Christou), so "of the Mashiach" is defensible.
    This is the ONE call the owner left explicitly open. Per-instance work is
    exactly what should settle it - adjudicate each genitive on its own sentence
    and report the pattern you found with your recommendation.
  - "Christian(s)" and "antichrist(s)" are kept (the VOT corpus uses them 53x
    and 16x respectively).


9. THE NATURALNESS GATE (the "no awkward translations" law)
-----------------------------------------------------------
This is the owner's headline requirement and the reason v1 was rejected. It is
enforced three ways: mechanically, by re-reading, and by test.

MECHANICAL - these patterns are FORBIDDEN in any verseOut and must be pinned by
a test that scans the emitted overlays. The existing corpus-wide guard in
restored-names.test.js already covers the first one; keep it and extend it.

  no determiner, adjective, or possessive immediately before HaMashiach:
      the|a|an|this|that|these|those|very|same|own|true|
      his|her|its|their|our|my|your|whose|which   +  HaMashiach
  no "of the HaMashiach"
  no doubled token: "HaMashiach HaMashiach", "Mashiach Mashiach", "YahuShua YahuShua"
  no circular gloss: a verse containing a translation formula
      ("which is translated", "which is to say", "being interpreted")
      with the SAME restored token on both sides of it
  no "the the", "a the", or any doubled article created by a substitution

BY RE-READING - for every changed verse, read verseOut start to finish as
English prose. Ask: would a reader who does not know Hebrew stumble here? Would
they have to re-read to parse the sentence? If yes, choose differently. Record
natural=pass. A row marked natural=fail may not ship; a row marked tension must
carry a rationale explaining the trade and is surfaced to the owner.

BY TEST - phase 3 adds guards to restored-names.test.js for every forbidden
pattern above, each RED-proven against a synthetic bad row before it goes green.

WORKED EXAMPLES, the shape of a right answer:

  KJV John 7:26  "...this is truly the very Christ?"
     WRONG  "the very HaMashiach"     stranded determiner, the original defect
     RIGHT  "the very Mashiach"       English prints "the very"; keep it intact

  "Thou art the Christ, the Son of the living God"
     RIGHT  "Thou art HaMashiach, the Son of the living God"
     ALSO OK "Thou art the Mashiach, ..."  - adjudicate which reads better here

  Matt 26:68 vocative  "Prophesy to us, Christ!"
     RIGHT  "Prophesy to us, Mashiach!"   direct address takes the bare form

  Luke 2:11 appositive "Christ the Lord"
     RIGHT  "Mashiach the Lord"           an article here would collide

  John 1:41 gloss  "We have found the Messias, which is, being interpreted, the Christ"
     RIGHT  "...found the Mashiach, which is, being interpreted, the Anointed"
            translate the gloss BY MEANING or the verse translates a word into itself


10. ANTI-BATCH GUARDS (how we prove this was per-instance work)
--------------------------------------------------------------
The owner has already received one delivery that was a rule dressed as an audit.
These guards exist so the claim is checkable, not merely asserted. Build them
into tools/validate-ledger.mjs and run it as part of every phase's proof.

  COVERAGE      ledger row count == 3,120, and every instance found in the
                source has a matching row id. Zero orphans in either direction.
  COMPLETENESS  zero rows with decision == null, natural == null, or an empty
                rationale, at the end of a slice.
  ROOTEDNESS    every rationale contains at least one substring of five or more
                consecutive words drawn from that verse's own before/after
                context. A rationale that cites nothing local is auto-flagged.
  NON-UNIFORMITY  report the distribution of rationale texts. Identical
                rationale strings repeated across many rows are a mechanical
                tell of batch-filling and must be investigated, not explained
                away. Near-duplicates are acceptable only where the sentences
                genuinely are near-duplicates, and the report must say so.
  VOCABULARY    every decision value is in the section 8 list. Any other value
                fails the run.
  SPOT AUDIT    at the end of each slice, re-open a random sample of 25 rows
                WITHOUT looking at the prior decision, re-adjudicate from the
                verse and the Greek, then compare. Report agreement rate and
                every disagreement with its resolution. An agreement rate below
                roughly 90 percent means the slice needs rework, not a note.

None of these guarantee good judgment. They guarantee that bad judgment cannot
hide behind a uniform output.
